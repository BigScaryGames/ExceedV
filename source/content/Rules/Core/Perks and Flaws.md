# What Are Perks?
Perks represent specific training, experiences, conditions or specializations your character has undergone. They embody the "how" and "why" behind your character's capabilities.

Perks cost XP and represent investments in specific areas of expertise. When you take a perk, you typically gain attribute points equal to the XP spent (some perks are free and some are without an attribute). These attribute points bring selected attributes closer to their next threshold (positive or negative).

Most perks don't have levels, but some do. In that case each level's effect is as the perk description says — see [[Conditioning Perks Overview]] for the leveled example: levels 1-4 each add +1 HP, level 5 consolidates into +1 Max Wound plus a capstone.

Perks may have prerequisites (other perks, skills, or attributes) and provide mechanical benefits that reflect your character's specialized training.

## Perk Categories

A perk's tags say what it is; its Cost line says which XP pays for it (`5 Battle XP` / `5 Life XP`).

- **Prowess Perks** (`#Prowess`): Fighting techniques, weapon training, tactical abilities — their XP advances the Prowess domain. Paid with Battle XP.
- **Magery Perks** (`#Magery`): Magical specializations and techniques — their XP advances the Magery domain. Paid with Battle XP.
- **Conditioning Perks** (`#Conditioning`): Staged HP progression. Paid with Battle XP.
- **Skill Perks** (`#Skill`): Specialized applications of life skills (they don't directly increase skill levels). Paid with Life XP.
- Battle-side perks that advance no domain (e.g. Plot Armor, the Predictive line) simply carry no discipline tag and a Battle XP cost.

## How Perks Work

1. Spend Battle XP or Life XP to purchase a perk
2. Choose which attribute receive the XP points (following the perk's specifications, if any)
3. The XP spent becomes attribute points that move the chosen attribute toward the next threshold
4. Example: Spend 5 XP on a battle perk, choose Strength → Strength gains 5 points toward +1 threshold

Perks follow the ability/effect system structure where abilities are active choices and effects are passive bonuses.
