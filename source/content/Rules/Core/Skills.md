## Skills System

Skills are the result of the life experience of your character. The result of dedication and/or talent and time spent mastering them.

## Reading a Skill Entry

A skill is written as **Medicine (H) (Field Surgery) [3]**.

| Part          | Meaning                                                                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **(H)**       | Complexity — Easy (E), Average (A), Hard (H): how costly the skill is to train. |
| **(Field Surgery)** | Focus — the field within the skill you have made yours.                         |
| **[3]**       | Level — your rank in the skill, 1 to 5.                                         |
> [!note] Note on Rank
> Some checks require a rank in a skill depending on the knoledge required to perform the check. GM decides if the penalty for the lack of said rank, and if the check is even possible without having skill rank for it.
> GM guidelines for is the complexity - Less complex skills get smaller penalty than more complex ones. (Rank per Easy, 2x per Average, 3x Per Complex)

Each skill also lists an **attribute pair** (e.g. Wit/Dexterity). The first attribute is the most common used attribute with the check, the default one. When asked to roll a check without specifying the attribute use that.

## Foci
When a skill reaches level 3 you and the GM decide your skill's Focus. What do you do best in said skill.  For that ask yourself (and confirm with GM) the appropriate foci.

Using medicine as an example - if you were training under one medic on the battlefield and helping him with surgeries of the wounded - you could take Surgeon of something making you a better Field Medic, but not for example a Focus that helps you run hospitals.
In some cases where the skill was one way exclusively one way through the game - e.g. all your negotiations were with traders, or or lied so much to everyone that no one is able to keep track of it GM might just decide for you the appropriate specialization instead.

**Changing your Focus.** Changing a Focus can be done with GM approval while you train a new level of the skill. 
Outside of advancement it takes half the time and training of your current tier level (see [[Training Rules]]).

Your new Focus replaces the old Signature. Perks that have prerequisites of Foci stay with you.

## **Perks and skills** 

Skill perk lists the skill level it requires (**Requirements:** [[Negotiating]] 2) and a few demand a specific Focus — otherwise Foci and perks are independent: your Focus is your skills identity, your perks - your extra training.

## A Life Skill

Not every human activity requires a skill attached. If you want a skill that lies outside the Focuses and categories - ask the GM if it is reasonable to add a livelihood skill.
Livelihood skills have no fixed attribute pair or complexity; the GM assigns them on creation. They can be used to earn [[Earning Income|Income]] they in normal circumstances.
They are not put in the same bucket as other, more adventuring related skills, for they are more focused on earning livelihood and rarely come up during adventuring or are plainly nieche. More often than not they might require a working facility (a farm, a smithy, a building to construct) or a specific setting (Courtroom, river, a town with customers).
Examples of skills by complexity.
1. **Easy Skills** - Mining, Logging, Construction, Skinning, Furnishing, Cooking, Blacksmithing, Fishing, Farming. Riding
2. **Average Skills** - Guarding, Tailor, Armoring, Weaponsmithing, Leatherworking, Masonry, Seafaring, Peddling.
3. **Complex Skills** - Lawyer, Accounting, Jewelcrafting, Tax collection, Painting (pictures, not walls), Sculpting, Poetry.

The examples are not limited to the list, when a craft is absent, ask the GM to determine its skill complexity and assign it the attributes.
Expressive art is usually in Professional Skills (H) category, but that requires GM clarification.
Craft perks pending a home: [[Gear Maintenence]] (gear upkeep), [[Lumberjack]] (Logging), [[Stiching a Patch]] (Furnishing).

## GM Notes — Ruling Skills

### Ruling Related Skills and Alternative Attributes
When a player states an approach or asks for a related skill:
1. Approve it, or say why it does not work here.
2. Set the DC for the approach as declared — a sound plan earns the easier number, a poor one the harder.
3. If a related skill is invoked, add its bonus: **full** for a strong pair, **half (round down)** for a loose one, **none** if it does not fit.

If the named skill is missing entirely, the check either falls back to the raw attribute (Easy and Average only) or you name another acceptable primary at a harder DC — Hard tasks still require the skill itself.
Default pairs, yours to overrule:
- **Full** — Engineering ↔ Arcane, Botany ↔ Medicine, Psychology ↔ Gossip, Streetwise ↔ Acting, Tracking ↔ Search.
- **Half** — any adjacent science, Negotiating ↔ Streetwise, Observation ↔ Search, Athletics ↔ Climbing.

### Designing Foci

A Signature takes one of four forms:
- **Aptitude** — +2 on checks directly within the field. The default. Most Foci stop here, and that likely means that its a placeholder.
- **Trade-off** — +N to checks within the field, −N to checks outside it. The flavorful ones (Vizier: +1 against the powerful, −1 against the have-nots). Players may propose custom trade-offs — approve or refuse.
- **Ability** — the Focus grants an Ability: a thing you do.
- **Effect** — the Focus grants an Effect: a change that is not an active action. Time cuts and assurances live here.

### Hard Skills at the Table
Hard-skill checks are walls: without the skill trained, many hard skills checks the check will be an automatic failure. GM should know the skills of the players - especially the hard ones to help create spotlight for said players. When players took said skills they likely did it because it would give them spotlight.

The bridge only one character can fix. The ritual only one can bind. The plea only one can make to a room of the powerful.
