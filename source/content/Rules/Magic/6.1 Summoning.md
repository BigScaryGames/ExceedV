> MS7 rules (first pass — pulled forward for playtesting; numbers are first-pass, tune at playtest).

Summoning creates **minions** — simplified creatures bound to your service by a spell. Minions are not characters and not companions: they are bodies of force (or freshly dead muscle) that occupy your Limit, act inside your turn, and unravel when they break. For a loyal companion that grows with you, see [[Bonded Companions]] — the two systems are deliberately different.

## The Rules

1. **A summon spell is both #Active and #Attuned.** You cast it — pay its AP, roll the [[Strain|Casting Check]] — and each summoned unit then occupies the spell's **Limit Cost** (1 slot per unit unless the spell says otherwise) until it unravels, is dismissed, or the spell ends.
2. **Minions are simplified statblocks.** Use a [[Bestiary]] entry or build one with the [[NPC and Encounter Builder|NPC builder]]. They run on the simplified sheet with one twist: **Stamina only, no Health.** Damage depletes Stamina; wounds, Consequence Rolls and dying don't apply to a body of force.
3. **They act on your turn.** Minions share your initiative — no separate slots. Each minion gets the **mook economy**: 1 move + 1 ability per turn. They have no reactions and never crit.
4. **They inherit your boons.** A minion benefits from #Boon conditions affecting its summoner (Grace, Inspired, Blessed, your auras) — what lifts you lifts them. Banes affecting a minion are applied normally; minions get no special protection.
5. **Unraveling.** At 0 Stamina the minion unravels — gone. Its Limit slot frees only after a **breather** (you cannot reallocate summon slots mid-fight; that's the anti-turtle pricing). Resummoning is simply casting the spell again later.
6. **Defending them.** Enemies attack a minion by rolling against its **static defenses** (players' enemies roll, minions don't). Minions defending players just present their DC.
7. **No stacking shenanigans.** One effect per source per zone ([[4.0 Lines and Zones]]); five skeletons is a skirmish line, not five buff platforms. Minions can't hold #Attuned effects or cast spells unless the spell explicitly says so.

## Scaling
Minion strength scales with **Magery** (which statblocks the spell can call) and **Limit** (how many you can hold at once: Limit = 3 + Will + Magery, minus your #Attuned effects). That's the whole balance: a swarm mage spends their entire Limit budget and inherits every buff tax that comes with it.

## The Necromancer Pattern
Raising the dead is deliberately cheap and dumb: a tier-1 spell ([[Summon Skelly Boys]]) produces mook-grade skeletons that eat your Limit. A necromancer doesn't need a perk tree — they need the spells and the Will to fill their Limit with the recently deceased. Elite undead (grave-wardens, bone knights) are tier-3+ spells summoning Threat 2 statblocks.

## Design Notes
- Counterplay is printed on the cards: minions are Stamina-only, inherit your banes, never crit, and die to one good #Burst. AoE is the swarm's answer, exactly as the original brainstorm assumed.
- Anti-turtle pricing: the Limit-slot-frees-after-a-breather rule means losing a summon mid-fight hurts your *buff capacity*, not just your board.
- Playtest knobs: mook economy vs 2 moves; whether inherit-boons should require the minion to be in your zone; Limit freeing instantly vs after breather.
