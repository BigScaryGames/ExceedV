> MS7 rules (first pass — pulled forward for playtesting; numbers are first-pass, tune at playtest).

A **bonded companion** is a creature bound to you by a perk — the ranger's wolf, the nazgûl's drake, the wizard's familiar. It is *not* a summon ([[6.1 Summoning]]): it doesn't eat Limit, doesn't unravel, and it grows with you. What it doesn't get is its own turn economy — **you** are its action economy.

## The Bond Rules

1. **No AP, no initiative.** While you are present and standing, the companion doesn't roll initiative and has no AP of its own. It acts on your turn, inside your turn, as you direct it.
2. **Commands cost your AP.** Commanding is not an action — you simply pay the command's AP cost from **your** AP pool as part of your turn, and the companion executes immediately. "The wolf attacks" costs the wolf's attack AP (from its statblock) out of your 5. Commands always cost **base AP** — domain AP (Prowess/Magery pools) cannot pay for them.
3. **Command Cap.** A companion can take at most **2 commands per turn** at Bond tier 1 — its total actions, including moves, never exceed this. The cap grows by +1 per tier perk: **3** with [[Companion Training]], **4** with [[Hardened Companion]], **5** with [[Legendary Bond]]. Your AP is the harder limit; the cap just keeps one creature from eating the whole party's spotlight.
4. **Scaling — Bond tier.** A companion's power scales with the **higher of your Magery or Prowess tier**. Companion perks spend Battle XP but advance **no domain**. Each [[Bestiary]] preset lists its Threat 1–3 numbers and an explicit Bond 4–5 upgrade line.
5. **Stamina only.** Like minions, companions use **Stamina only, no Health** — no wound tracking for a pet. At 0 Stamina the **Bond Breaks**: the companion is out of the fight (fled, unconscious, banished). It recovers with a night's rest or better; it only dies if the fiction says so (GM's call, and a kind one at that).
6. **Boons flow down the bond.** A companion inherits #Boon conditions affecting you, exactly like a minion ([[6.1 Summoning]]). Banes apply to it normally.
7. **Reactions.** None by default. Perks may grant the companion one reaction per round.
8. **When you're gone.** If you are unconscious, dead, or separated, the companion acts on its own: 3 AP on its own initiative slot, driven by its statblock's instinct line — guard your body, flee with you, avenge you. The GM runs it, and self-preservation wins ties.

## Commanding in Practice
- **Move / Engage / Disengage / Stand:** pay the companion's cost as listed in its statblock (usually 1 AP).
- **Attack:** pay the attack's AP (2–3 AP usually); roll `2d10 + bond attack bonus` against the target's defense, per [[NPC and Encounter Builder]] rolling-NPC rules. It can crit on doubles.
- **Abilities:** statblock abilities and perk-granted commands ([[Intercept]], [[Harass]]) list their AP.
- You may interleave freely: attack, command the wolf to attack, cast, command again — until your AP or the Command Cap runs out.

## Design Notes
- The zero-sum is the balance: every command is AP you didn't spend on yourself. A companion build trades personal output for board presence — the same trade a Limit-swarm mage makes, paid in a different currency.
- The Command Cap (2→5) exists so the bond can't quietly become a second character sheet with better action economy than yours.
- Playtest knobs: whether commands should cost +1 AP (a "handler tax") if commanding feels too cheap; whether the cap should count moves at half.
