**Participants:** Game Master, Bram (player), Yara (player), Cobb (player)
**Format:** Post-session debrief. The GM puts the grievance hat in the middle of the table. Raw, in-persona system feedback; the AI's own numbers and analysis live in [[Playtest Statistics]] and [[System Analysis - Findings]].

---

## The GM opens

**Game Master:** "Rules of the debrief: one thing you loved, one thing that fought you, one thing you'd change. Numbers welcome, feelings mandatory. Bram, you've had your spreadsheet open since round two, so — you first."

## Bram (player) — the optimizer

**Loved:**
- "The action economy is genuinely good. Seven AP with two of them domain-locked meant every turn had a *budget*, not just an action and a bonus action. Calf Strike into a held guard into Shield Warden — that's a turn with a plan. I never once had dead AP I couldn't spend on *something* useful."
- "Rolling my own defense kept me in the game on enemy turns. When Pike swung and I rolled doubles? Best moment of my night, and it was on a *defense*. That's rare."
- "The ambush rules are crisp. One leader roll, stunned losers, done. We ambushed *them* with it and I still think it's fair."

**Fought me:**
- "The attribute threshold economy produces shelf purchases. Cobb finished at 29 Dexterity points — one purchase from +2, forever. Yara had two attributes at 28 and 29. If the answer is 'buy more later,' the system should say so, because right now it just feels like the machine kept my change."
- "Perk requirements referencing attribute *values* — Shield Rush wanted Strength 1, Backstab wants Dexterity 2 — are unreachable at 50 XP without gutting your domain. Those perks are printed on the menu and locked in the kitchen."
- "Mook initiative vs mook health is mis-tuned. I lost an entire round to wolves that died before my turn. One HP means the fast killers delete the encounter and the slow tank swings at air. Mooks need either more HP or something to *do* — shove, grab, block — so their turn matters even when they die fast."

**Would change:**
- "Critical defenses do *nothing* by default. I rolled doubles on Deflect twice — the rules say 'succeeds beautifully' and then leave the room. Give me something: recover AP, stagger the attacker, a free Disengage, anything. Right now it's applause."
- "And write down what a critical *failure* on an attack does. 'If applicable' is not a rule, it's a shrug. Cobb invented two different consequences for the same result because the book wouldn't."

## Yara (player) — the narrative player

**Loved:**
- "The Attuned-versus-Active split is the most *witchy* magic design I've played. My charms live in the Limit like trinkets on a shelf, my big spells are rituals with risk. The Ghost Sound critical extending the duration — a minute of increasingly committed phantom goats — is exactly the kind of payoff I play for. More of that. Put crit-cast refinements on more spells."
- "The two XP pools made my gossip hour in the tavern feel *paid*. I got Life XP for working the room. In most games the social scene is the snack before the fight; here it was a shift at my actual job."
- "The ending worked because the system let it: Aid action, opposed negotiation, a morale condition on the villain. We finished a boss *by talking* and the mechanics never blinked. That's the system at its best."

**Fought me:**
- "Twenty-three spells at character creation. Twenty-three. I'm a teacher, I mark lists for a living, and I could not tell you what's on my own list. There's no 'prepared spells' concept, no loadout — it's the whole menu every fight. I want to *choose six* and love them."
- "Strain never happened. Not once. My casting bonus is +5 against a DC 8 or 10 — I cannot fail a tier 0 or 1 spell in any meaningful way. The system's whole magic risk economy — fail, strain, wounds — never entered play. Either the DC floor rises or low-tier spells can't be trivial. Right now the kettle has no price."
- "I forgot my Limit twice mid-fight and nobody noticed, because nothing was tracking it. Three attuned of seven — that's a physical dial waiting for a designer. And *Stamina doesn't come back between fights?* I finished the session at 8 Health with an empty Stamina pool and no rule to refill it short of a night."

**Would change:**
- "Spell attacks against static NPC defenses crit constantly. I crit-cast three times — the beat-by-10 extended rule was made for rolls that *contested*, and against a fixed 12 it fires every time I roll well. Cap it, or make NPC defenses roll when the attack is a spell."

## Cobb (player) — the casual veteran

**Loved:**
- "The bow *feels like a bow*. Aim, range increments, the Distance DC, Fast Archer letting me aim while I move — I have never had ranged combat flow like that. Two shots, one kill, reposition. Chef's kiss."
- "Skirmisher's Step — throw and slip out free — is my whole fantasy in one line. Zones and skirmishes did the 'who's near who' math I usually hate about maps."
- "Morale on the statblock. The bowman *ran*. The toll-boy *ran*. I've played systems where enemies fight to the last dice roll and it's stupid every time. This table's world felt alive."

**Fought me:**
- "Two critical failures in one fight, both doubles, both on misses that were already missing. Rolling 2-and-2 to fail *and* then being told it's ALSO worse? That's the dice kicking me twice. If crit fails exist, give me a complication, not a punishment — string snaps, fine, but make what happens next *interesting*."
- "The numeric-advantage math in a skirmish — minus one per extra enemy, back-to-back at three allies — is the GM doing arithmetic mid-swing. I couldn't help Yara track why she was at −1. It's one more thing to count in a system that's already proud of its counting."
- "Kleptomania — my flaw, my pride — never triggered. Nothing unattended all session except a *warded* strongbox. The flaw rules need a 'the GM will put a shiny thing in the room' clause or they're just negative XP with good flavor."

**Would change:**
- "Mooks. One HP boys with d4 sticks swung once all night and then died or fled. Either give them a grab, a scream that Shakens, a shove off the mill steps — *anything* — or fold them into the leader's turn so they're at least fast scenery."

## The Game Master's own debrief

**What worked at my end of the table:**
- "The NPC and Encounter Builder is the best prep tool this system has. Twenty minutes, five statblocks, and the threat math *held*: party weight six against roughly six threat across two waves felt like a hard-won professional job, not a video game. The static-defense convention made my side of the screen fast."
- "Lines and Zones ran cleaner than any grid system I've GM'd. I sketched four zones on a napkin, wrote two line costs, done. Players argued about the *fiction* of positioning, never the mechanics of it. That's the win condition for this milestone."

**What fought me:**
- "Five house rules before the first dice hit the table, ten gaps logged in Session 0. The big ones: spell-attack resolution (I ruled casting check = attack roll), surprise stun thresholds, unencumbered-vs-armor, stamina recovery between fights, critical-failure effects. None of it broke the game; all of it *will* vary table to table, which for a system this far along is the real finding."
- "Yara's crit-cast rate is a balance alarm. Three critical casts, every one via beat-by-10 against static defenses, one of them rolling 4d6 for 7 damage — the math says 'spells too strong' and the dice said 'spells too strong, embarrassingly rolled.' The magic chapter already apologizes for this. It's right to."
- "The Bellow — flat DC 14 Resolve — Shook two of three players on coin-flip dice. Morale conditions hitting the *whole* table from one NPC action is swingy at this tier. I'd want a cap or a smaller penalty at Threat 2."
