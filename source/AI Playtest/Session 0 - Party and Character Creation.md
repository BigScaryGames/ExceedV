**Participants:** Game Master, Bram (player), Yara (player), Cobb (player)
**Purpose:** Party formation and character creation for the Exceed v0.6 playtest one-shot.
**Starting budget:** 50 Battle XP + 50 Life XP per character ("trained adventurers, not novices"). Flaw limit: up to ~1/3 of points per pool.

---

## Opening — the GM frames the game

**Game Master:** "Right. Three of you, one-shot, system playtest. You're competent freelancers out of the city of Hexgate — enough of a reputation that villages hire you, not enough that anyone remembers your name. Before we build characters: this system has no classes. You are what you buy. Two wallets: Battle XP buys fighting technique, magic, and toughness. Life XP buys everything else — skills, social clout, street craft. Spend accordingly."

**Bram (player):** "Two currencies. Fine. What's the exchange rate?"

**Game Master:** "There isn't one. They're earned separately too — fights pay Battle, everything else pays Life. That's one of the things we're testing."

**Yara (player):** "So if I want to play someone who talks to spirits and knows every gossip in a ten-mile radius…"

**Game Master:** "…that's Life XP, and your fireballs are Battle XP. The split *is* the class system, softly."

## Party composition talk

**Cobb (player):** "Standard triangle, then? Someone holds the door, someone throws fire, someone hides in a bush?"

**Bram (player):** "Defense wins fights in systems with good action economies. I'll hold the door. Shield and spear, ex-watch. I want to find out what defending actually buys here — the GM knows why."

**Game Master:** "I do. Nobody else take a shield, then — redundant Deflect bonuses don't stack."

**Yara (player):** "I'll take the magic wallet. A hedge witch. Healer, mostly — I want the spells to feel like craft, not artillery. If I wanted artillery I'd play your wargames, Bram."

**Bram (player):** "Take at least one damage spell. Trust me on the action math — a healer with zero offense is a spectator in round one."

**Yara (player):** "One. *One* fireball-shaped thing, and I'm naming it."

**Cobb (player):** "Bush it is. Scout and bow. Twenty years of rogues, I'm not stopping now."

**Bram (player):** "Take Lockpicking and Stealth, yes, but someone also needs to notice things. Perception feeds initiative and Dodge in this system — scout with bad Perception is a trap build."

**Cobb (player):** "…I hate that you're right. Fine. Observation, Tracking, the works."

## Attribute education (the GM explains the bottom-up economy)

**Game Master:** "Quick, because this trips everyone: you cannot buy attributes. Every XP you spend on a skill, perk or spell also drops points into one of the attributes listed on that purchase — your choice at purchase. Ten points in one attribute makes it +1. Thirty makes it +2. Attributes cap at +5."

**Yara (player):** "So my witch's Willpower grows because I *bought spells that train will*, not because I pressed the Willpower button."

**Game Master:** "Exactly. Skills and perks drive attributes, attributes drive the rolls."

**Bram (player):** "And flaws run it in reverse — Kleptomania's four points *off* Will. Cobb, don't."

**Cobb (player):** "It's four XP and it's *funny*."

**Bram (player):** "It's four XP and your Will pool is nearly empty already."

**Game Master:** "Flaw limit says he can. My ruling: allowed, but the flaw gets *played*. Cobb — Kleptomania, yes or no."

**Cobb (player):** "Yes. Obviously yes."

## Session 0 rulings and gaps (GM's sticky note)

Things the GM had to invent, clarify, or flag while building characters:

1. **Ruling — "Medicine 1/2":** Minor Healing and Major Healing require a skill called Medicine, which no longer exists in the 0.6 skill list. Mapped to **Medical Science**. *(Gap: legacy requirement names on spells.)*
2. **Ruling — learning spells at creation:** Learning spells formally needs a teacher or Magical Theory ≥ tier, plus one day per XP. At creation the GM hand-waved "your backstory teachers". *(Gap: no explicit character-creation rule for starting spells.)*
3. **Ruling — starting gear and money:** Rules specify no starting wealth. GM kit: one armor, weapons of choice, a tool kit, 20g coin each, plus a 30g party fund. *(Gap: starting equipment undefined.)*
4. **Finding — Backstab is unreachable:** Backstab requires Prowess 2 *and* Dexterity 2. Dexterity 2 means 30 attribute points; Prowess 2 means 30 domain XP. At 50 Battle XP both are impossible together. Cobb deferred it to advancement. *(Balance/gating finding.)*
5. **Ruling — bow attack attribute:** The weapon table says "Ranged: Dexterity or Perception based on weapon" without saying which. Ruling: bows use either, pick per attack (matches the Bows Training attribute pair Dexterity/Perception).
6. **Ruling — damage dice:** Two places describe damage-dice scaling differently (one by "skill" level, one by Prowess tier). Ruling: **Prowess tier**, per the Damage Calculation file. *(Wording gap.)*
7. **Finding — Attribute thresholds create near-misses:** With 100 XP total, characters keep landing at 8–9 or 12–14 points in secondary attributes — one purchase short of the +1. Bram's Agility pool finished at exactly 9. Feels deliberate; also feels a little mean. *(Feel finding.)*
8. **Finding — Alarming Wards does not exist:** The magic chapter's Tier 1 table lists Alarming Wards, but there is no spell file for it. Avoided. *(Broken reference in rules.)*
9. **Ruling — Conditioning cost table:** The Conditioning progression table's "Total XP Cost" column reads as cumulative (worked example confirms); each level costs Max_Wounds XP at 2 Max Wounds. Cheap HP; noted for balance review.
10. **Finding — odd remainders:** Neither Bram nor Yara could spend exactly 50 Battle XP without buying something useless; both banked 1 XP. Cobb hit exactly 50 (plus 4 flaw XP into Life). *(Point-buy granularity finding.)*
