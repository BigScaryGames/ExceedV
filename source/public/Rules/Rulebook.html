    Intro

    Intro

    Start here: what Exceed is and how to read the rules.

    1. Welcome to Exceed

    Exceed is a skill-based fantasy tabletop role-playing game using polyhedral dice. The Game Master, like in most TTRPGs sets the scenes, and represents the characters who are not the players (NPCs), the monsters, and the world as a whole. Game is designed to be played in group of three to six players, but should work with 1-2 players as well.

    Core Philosophy of the game can be best observed in how it handles Character Development, Magic and many other mechanics in the game. e.g. Bottom-Up Character Development: Instead of saying “You’re strong, so you’re good at athletics,” Exceed asks “Why are you strong?”, instead of saying “You are good with combat so your good with swinging.” it asks “What maneuvers and training made you good?“.

    Character growth follows logical story progression: training something narrow makes you better at adjacent things and builds pockets of specialization that allow you to exceed the narrow confines of many other systems. A skilled swordsman won’t be utterly useless given another weapon, unless you want him to, of course.

    The game emphasizes player agency, tactical depth, and character development connected to the character progression. Rather than starting with predetermined classes putting you on a rails, the characters grow organically during the games through learning perks, getting flaws and rising through organizations to something you were not expecting them to be.

    Flow

    Game is played in sessions, and is designer to work for campaigns spreading multiple sessions, and isn’t well designed for one-shot adventures due to the crunchy nature of its systems. It requires some background calculations to remove the tracking that comes with the system, that is why, using an application or VTT (when that will be available in the future) is advised.

    Tools

    The game doesn’t provide printed rules and character sheets, using digital ones instead, currently publicly hosted online. Thus a device is required.

    For a in-person game a set of dice including two ten sided dice are required, preferably for each player, but dice rollers are fine as well. For combat it is preferred to have miniatures, but theater of mind and vtt are or will be viable as well.

    In future iterations, distance might be counted using a ruler as main distance calculation option, but that is up to playtesting.

    Game for a few

    In Exceed we believe in gate-keeping the fun, because when something belongs to everyone - it belongs to no one. This game belongs to me. This game allows diversity of builds and play-styles, it does not engage in modern identity-based themes and contemporary social commentary neither in its mechanics, nor in the narratives. When it does - it follows the classical narratives and eschews post-modern ones. It is obvious from its game design, but I am just spelling it out.

    If you feel uncomfortable - leave, there are games for Everyone out there. This isn’t one.

    Ready to play?

    Get your phones/tablets/laptops/PCs out and proceed to the Basic rules. Go through them in an ordered manner.

    Critical Results

    Critical Results and Critical Hits

    Critical Results

    Doubles on the kept dice modify outcomes. With advantage you choose which 2 of the 3 dice to keep — so you may keep a matching pair on purpose. With disadvantage you must keep the 2 lowest dice. On a normal roll, both dice count.

    • Success + Doubles: Critical Success
    • Failure + Doubles: Critical Failure (if applicable)

    Critical Hits

    Critically succeeding against an enemy with an attack or spell roll creates a critical hit or critical defense. By default - i.e. without modifications from perks and spells - only critical hits provide with extra effects - in this case extra damage. Perks in the game allow to add effects and activate abilities on own critical defense or enemy’s critical failures.

    As of MS5 critical hits double damage dice. In later iterations weapons will be provided critical modifiers using CritX (where X is the extra damage per damage die).

    3.2 Perks and Flaws

    What Are Perks?

    Perks represent specific training, experiences, conditions or specializations your character has undergone. They embody the “how” and “why” behind your character’s capabilities.

    Perks cost XP and represent investments in specific areas of expertise. When you take a perk, you typically gain attribute points equal to the XP spent (some perks are free and some are without an attribute). These attribute points bring selected attributes closer to their next threshold (positive or negative).

    Most perks don’t have levels, but some do. In that case each previous level overrides each next level, as per perk description.

    Example of overriding are: Conditioning perks - Conditioning Perks Overview

    Perks may have prerequisites (other perks, skills, or attributes) and provide mechanical benefits that reflect your character’s specialized training.

    Perk Categories

    • Combat Perks: Specialized combat techniques, weapon training, tactical abilities
    • Skill Perks: Specialized applications of skills (though skill perks don’t directly increase skill levels)
    • Spellcraft Perks: Magical specializations and techniques

    How Perks Work

    1. Spend Combat XP or Skill XP to purchase a perk
    2. Choose which attribute receive the XP points (following the perk’s specifications, if any)
    3. The XP spent becomes attribute points that move the chosen attribute toward the next threshold
    4. Example: Spend 5 XP on a combat perk, choose Might → Might gains 5 points toward +1 threshold

    Perks follow the ability/effect system structure where abilities are active choices and effects are passive bonuses.

    Flaws

    Introduction to Flaws (to be integrated in 0.6)

    Flaws are perks with a minus sign.

    They represent personal, social and physical limitations that shape your character. Like perks, they follow the bottom-up philosophy: “Why are you disadvantaged in this area?”

    Perhaps you are an alcoholic that reduces your perception or wit (not yet written might have other attributes), an egoist that reduces your will or charisma, or you were an adventurer before you got an arrow to the knee making you less agile. Flaws provide negative XP that can be spent on other perks, skills and spells. When you take a flaw, you assign its negative XP to decrease chosen attributes and give yourself the appropriate XP.

    How Flaws Work

    1. Select a flaw and receive negative XP (e.g., -5, -10, -15 XP)
    2. Choose which attribute receive the negative XP points (if there is a choice)
    3. The negative XP moves the chosen attribute toward the previous threshold
    4. Example: Take a flaw worth -5 XP, choose Will. Will was at 3XP prior → Will loses 5 points and becomes -2, thus turning the stat into -1.

    Flaws create mechanical effects beyond just attribute penalties (roleplay hooks, situational limitations).

    Voluntary vs. Involuntary Flaws

    • Voluntary Flaws: Chosen during character creation for negative XP. They provide you with experience points to spend.
    • Involuntary Flaws: Acquired through story events, GM decisions, or consequences. They might provide with XP points up to GM discretion.

    Both affect attributes similarly, but only voluntary ones provide XP during character creation and involuntary ones are up to GM.

    Flaws Guidelines

    This guideline is mainly for design purposes.

    Intro into Flaw Design

    The basic formula for calculating is Severity × Frequency. It is calculated in different ways based on flaw type.

    There are 5 flaw types. This doesn’t automatically put them intoSkill orCombat categories for XP, so tags will remain present.

    1. Addictions
    2. Stigmas
    3. Personality
    4. Bodily
    5. Supernatural

    Chance of appearance is a hidden GM roll on chances of issue coming to play during game if applicable, as per GM. This doesn’t count player’s play-style or what they are going to do, so it’s an approximation, as well as a hidden roll at the start of session to see if it will come up if applicable.

    Resisting a flaw is a flat d10 roll that is only modified by circumstances by GM and not stats. That resist roll already considers character’s willpower, their endurance and other stats and perks. You have to roll above DC to resist.

    Personality

    Personality flaws are internal compulsions, biases, and emotional vulnerabilities. These include gambling, kleptomania, cowardice, greed, compulsive lying, phobias, and other behavioral issues that character struggles to control.

    Personality flaws don’t always carry attribute penalties, but when they do, it reflects deep-rooted psychological or physiological damage.

    The formula for Personality flaws is Frequency × Severity.

    Frequency Calculation

    Frequency = (Average appearance of trigger) + (Resist DC / 10)

    ComponentRangeDescription
    Average appearance of trigger0.1 to 1.0How often relevant situations arise in campaign
    Resist DC / 100.1 to 1.0How hard to resist when triggered (DC 2 = 0.2, DC 8 = 0.8)

    Frequency Range: 0.2 to 2.0

    Severity Calculation

    Severity = (Consequences/Legality) + (Compulsion severity) + (Triggered effects)

    ComponentRangeDescription
    Consequences/Legality1 to 3Legal/social severity - administrative punishment (1), criminal (2), severe (3)
    Compulsion severity1 to 3Urge strength when resisted - mild (1), moderate (2), overwhelming (3)
    Triggered effects1 to 3What happens when you give in - minor (1), significant (2), severe (3)

    Severity Range: 3 to 9

    Examples

    Kleptomania

    Compulsion to steal small, non-essential items when opportunity presents itself.

    Frequency:

    • Trigger appears when shopping and items are poorly placed = 0.5
    • Can resist 8/10 times (DC 8) = 0.2 (2/10 chance to fail)
    • Frequency Sum: 0.5 + 0.2 = 0.7

    Severity:

    • Legality: 2 (illegal, but administrative punishment, not death penalty)
    • Consequences: 3 (reputation loss, possible stigma)
    • Drawbacks/Effects: 1 (minor stigma if caught)
    • Severity Sum: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6

    Result: 0.7 × 6 = 4.2 ≈ 4 XP

    Effects:

    • Attribute: -4 points in Will (compulsion overrides rational decision)
    • When triggered by opportunity to steal, must roll above DC 8 or make attempt to steal within one turn or feel increasingShaken

    Bodily

    Bodily flaws represent physical disabilities, injuries, or limitations. These include missing limbs, organ damage, sensory impairment, chronic pain, and other permanent physical conditions.

    These are the most straightforward flaws in terms of pricing - they typically include an attribute penalty reflective of the actual physical limitation, plus situational penalties.

    GM will look at 2 factors.

    1. Attribute impact - Which attributes are affected and to what degree. (-5 to -20 XP)
    2. Situational limitations - What character cannot do, or what they do at penalty. (-5 to -20 XP)

    E.g. One-Eyed. Missing one eye. Depth perception severely affected, peripheral vision gone. Attribute impact: -10 points in Perception (-10 XP) Situational: -2 toStrike attacks in melee combat and all checks relying on sight (ranged attacks, Perception checks) (-10 XP) Total: -20 XP

    E.g. Crippled Leg. Leg damaged such that walking is painful, running impossible. Attribute impact: -15 points in Agility (-15 XP) Situational: Cannot Run action, -1 AP per turn, -2 on Balance/Acrobatics checks (-10 XP) Total: -25 XP

    E.g. Missing Arm. One arm missing entirely. Cannot two-hand weapons, cannot use shields, off-hand actions impossible. Attribute impact: -15 points in Dexterity (fine motor control reduced) (-15 XP) Situational: Cannot use weapons requiring two hands, cannot use shields, -2 on all tasks requiring two hands (carrying, climbing) (-15 XP) Total: -30 XP

    Supernatural

    Supernatural flaws are curses, magical afflictions, unnatural traits, and conditions that exist beyond normal biology. These include curses, lycanthropy, vampirism, born magical susceptibility, pact-bound limitations, and otherworldly taints.

    These flaws are often the most severe due to their unpredictable nature, visibility, and potential for escalation.

    GM will look at 4 factors.

    1. Origin - Was it chosen (pact, ritual) or inflicted (curse, born)? Inflicted flaws are worth more.
    2. Visibility - Is it concealed or obvious? Visible flaws carry social stigma automatically.
    3. Control - Does character have agency over it, or does it act against them?
    4. Effects - What does it do mechanically?

    Base price 10, adjusted by factors.

    E.g. Witch-Blood. Born with visible signs of magical affinity. In superstitious communities, this is dangerous.     E.g. Cursed by Witch. Hexed to always be slightly unlucky. Origin: Inflicted (×1.5) Visibility: Concealed (×0.5) Control: Acts against character (×1.5) Effects: Once per day, must re-roll a natural 10 and take second result (which may be lower) 10 × 1.5 × 0.5 × 1.5 = -11.25 ≈ -10 XP Attribute: -10 points in Perception (things just seem to happen) Effects: Bad Luck once per day

    E.g. Pact with Dark Entity. Sold something (voice, shadow, firstborn) for power. Origin: Chosen (×0.5) Visibility: Concealed usually, but entity may manifest (×1) Control: Contractual obligations (×1.5) Effects: Must perform services for entity periodically, entity can claim payment at any time 10 × 0.5 × 1 × 1.5 = -7.5 ≈ -8 XP Attribute: -10 points in Will (bound to another) Effects: Debt owed to [entity], entity can compel services

    Range Increment Calculation

    Each weapon defines its own Range Increment in meters (e.g., a shortbow might have 15m increments, while a longbow has 25m).

    To determine your range increment:

    SituationRange Increment
    Target is in the same Skirmish as you0 (point blank)
    Target is in the same zone but not in your skirmish1
    Target is in a different zoneceil(Total Distance / Weapon Range Increment)

    Total Distance: Sum of line distances from your zone to the target’s zone.

    Example

    • Your longbow has a 25m range increment
    • Target is in zone C, you are in zone A
    • Lines: A→B is 15m, B→C is 15m
    • Total Distance = 30m
    • Range Increment = ceil(30 / 25) = 2
    • Distance DC = 14

    4.0 Lines and Zones

    The maps in Exceed are defined in Lines and Zones. Maps can be used with or without a grid, as distances are tracked with lines — no map at all, or a map on a napkin, works as well as any other map.

    A creature’s position amounts to two things: its whereabouts (what Zone it is in), and who it’s contending or shielding with in melee (its Skirmish).

    Zones

    Zones are abstract areas — a room, an alleyway, a hilltop, a city square. The GM decides the zoning based on the needs of the scene.

    Within a zone, you can get into a Skirmish with an enemy or an ally with a single Engage action, followed by an attack or other offensive ability.

    When you Engage an enemy or an ally — or they engage you — you engage all the targets in that Skirmish.

    Skirmish

    A group in a zone that are in melee range with each other. A single character can’t be in 2 engagements at the same time.

    Strength in numbers

    Unless a zone states otherwise having a numerical disadvantage within a skirmish provides -1 to deflect and dodge rolls per difference to the defender capped at -4. The cap applies to the numeric-advantage penalty only — other penalties (e.g. Off-Guard) stack beyond it.

    Back-to-back: if you have at least 3 allies in your skirmish (or count as having 3 allies), you no longer suffer the numeric disadvantage on defense.

    Example: 2 guards skirmish 5 bandits — each guard deflects and dodges at -3 (outnumbered 5 to 2). Two more guards join: each of the 4 guards now has 3 allies in the skirmish, so back-to-back applies and they defend with no numeric penalty despite still being outnumbered.

    Reach and Range weapons allow attacking without entering the skirmish, the attacks count the attacker for the benefit of numeric advantage for that attack.

    To disengage from the Skirmish you need to perform a Disengage action. Trying to Leave the Skirmish with or without a Disengage action can interact with an ability of an effect.

    Tags:Combat

    Link to original

    Lines

    Lines represent the distance between zones. Each line has a Distance statistic and can hold effects.

    Line of sight: blocking line of sight is a property of a line (a wall, a corner, thick smoke). Generally, whether a zone can be seen from another is up to the GM.

    Cost of traversal = Distance × Multipliers from Effects AP to Move = ceil(Cost / Speed)

    You must have the full AP cost available to cross — there is no partial movement along a line. GMs are advised to write the costs on the map next to each line to keep track of the effects.

    ! Rule of thumb Lines should cost no more than 20 in the late game and no more than 10 in the early game. When designing a map, no zone should be further than about 2/3 of a turn of Move from its neighbors on average.

    To calculate distance between disconnected zones reference Distance in Combat.

    Effects

    Lines and Zones can hold effects that modify the rules — providing effects, abilities and alike.

    Environmental, situational or magical — some can be removed, suppressed or bypassed by abilities explicitly stating so, or with checks and improvisation approved by the GM.

    Examples: Line is a wall, requiring DC 15 climbing check and distance 10. “Hey GM, I want to cast levitate on the group” / “Hey GM, I want to be a big guy and help others climb by jump-starting them” / “Hey GM, can I throw my rope dart and climb that way” “Cast the spell / Lower the DC by 5 if you spend your whole turn on this / Add an extra AP, and reduce DC by 3.”

    Defaults on Effects in Lines and Zones Effects.

    Setting Up an Encounter

    Encounter Setup

    When combat breaks out, the GM sets the initial state of the encounter in three steps: draw the map, place the combatants, roll Initiative.

    1. Draw the Map

    Split the scene into Zones (rooms, alleyways, hilltops — whatever fits the fiction) and connect them with Lines. Give every line a Distance and write it on the map. Add zone and line effects as needed — see Lines and Zones Effects.

    Rule of thumb: 2–5 zones covers most fights. A single zone is fine for a brawl; more zones reward ranged and mobile builds.

    2. Place the Combatants

    Every combatant starts in the zone the fiction puts them in.

    • Default: Nobody starts in a Skirmish. Opposing sides start in different zones, or in the same zone out of melee range.
    • Fight breaks out up close (a tavern argument turns violent, a parley goes wrong): the GM places the involved combatants directly into a skirmish.
    • Zone effects apply immediately: a Crowded zone puts everyone in it into one skirmish from the start.
    • Ambush: The ambushed side is placed first, where their route or activity puts them. The ambushing side then picks its starting zones (and may start hidden). See Surprise Rounds for the initiative effects.

    3. Roll Initiative

    Roll Initiative and begin the first round.

    Tokens on a Tactical Map

    Token position inside a zone does not represent real positioning — only the Zone and Skirmish state matter. As a convention: put your token on your side of the zone, and group tokens that share a skirmish together, each on their side’s edge of the cluster.

    4.1 Taking Damage

    Players take damage as per Health and Wounds first depleting stamina, then depleting health. Players can reach negative HP with a value of Max Health not accounted for wounds.

    Getting Below 0 HP and Death

    Getting to 0 Health: Each time your Health reaches or goes below 0, roll a Consequence Roll , and mark 1 wound.

    When at or below 0 Health you need to roll Endure vs Treatment DC of your highest treatment DC wound to stay conscious at the start of each of your turns.

    • Each lethal hit you take in this state makes you roll a Consequence Roll.
    • Each non-lethal hit you take in this state makes you roll Endure against the hit to stay conscious.

    When below negative full Health - you fall unconscious without a check. Unless the attack was nonlethal you also need to roll Endure vs Treatment DC to stay alive at the end of each of your turns.

    Death: If your lethal wounds would exceed max wounds or reach negative HP due to a lethal attack, you die. If your overall wounds (including non-lethal) exceed your max wounds you go unconcious.

    Wound System

    For the full wound and treatment system, see 4.2 Wounds And Consequences.

    Medical Treatment and Wound Consequences

    Getting into a fight is a dangerous endeavor, and you haven’t emerged from it unscathed.

    Core Wound System

    1. Roll the [Consequence Roll]
    2. Write down the lasting consequence on your character sheet and mark 1 wound
    3. Remove the mark from the wound and the consequence after the rest period. See |Recovery Time by Quality for conditions based rest time modification.

    Treatment Methods

    First Aid

    Medical skill check against Treatment DC

    • Trained: 4 actions to perform
    • Untrained: 3 rounds, cannot be performed during combat
    • Note: Exiting combat to perform first aid prevents dodging, and deflecting
    • Effect: Stops bleeding, prevents or delays escalation

    • Enhanced treatment: +2 bonus (Breather) or +4 bonus (Shift)

    • Base Time: 1 Shift
    • Enhanced treatment: +2 for 2 Shifts, +4 for 4 Shifts
    • Effect: Required for Severity 3 consequences to heal

    • Serious: Season

    5. Damage Riders — Musings (temp design, no DOTs)

    Exceed does not use ticking damage-over-time. Most combats last about 3 rounds — a DOT that ticks twice is just damage with bookkeeping. Lingering harm takes one of these forms instead:

    1. Front-loaded damage — deal it now.
    2. One-time riders — conditions that trigger once on the target’s next qualifying event, then clear (duration preset 1):
      • Bleed X: the next time the target spends AP on a movement action (Engage, Disengage, Move, Stand Up), it takes X damage, then Bleed clears. First Aid clears it without harm.
      • Fragile X: the next instance of damage the target takes deals +X, then Fragile clears. (cracked armor, opened guard)
      • Pinned X: (arrows, spears) the target’s next movement action costs +X AP, or it spends 1 AP to remove the projectile. Then clears.
    3. Map effects — persistent harm lives on the map, not on tokens: set the zone on fire, fill it with poison gas (Hazardous zone — damage or an Endure roll at the start of a turn spent in it). The GM tracks one zone, not five creatures. This is also the long-fight answer: don’t DOT the boss — set his throne room on fire.
    4. Out of combat — poisons, disease and untreated wounds run through Fatigue and the Consequences system, not combat conditions.

    Same condition applied twice: keep the highest X, refresh the trigger.

    4.4 Weapons and Combat Training

    Combat skill in Exceed is determined by one’s Martial Domain.

    To advance in the Combat Domain, you must learn combat perks. Weapon-specific combat perks require Weapon Training - proficiency in a weapon category gained through training perks with theWeaponTraining trait.

    Weapon Categories:

    • Brawling - Fists, kicks, grappling
    • Shield - All shields
    • Blades - Knives, swords, daggers, sabres (slashing/piercing edged weapons)
    • Ax - Axes, picks, cleavers (chopping weapons)
    • Impact - Clubs, hammers, maces, flails (bludgeoning weapons)
    • Polearms - Spears, staves, halberds, pikes, glaives (long reach weapons)
    • Bows - Bows, crossbows (string-drawn ranged)
    • Thrown - Darts, javelins, throwing axes, slings (hand-propelled ranged)

    Weapon Training Perks: Each weapon category has a corresponding training perk that costs 5 XP. These perks grant basic proficiency (removing the -2 untrained penalty) and provide a unique tactical ability specific to that weapon category.

    Untrained Penalty: Using a weapon without training in its category imposes a -2 competence penalty to all rolls.

    Martial Progression

    Martial advances through XP spent on combat perks (including weapon training). All such XP accumulates toward the tier thresholds — the domain is never bought directly:

    TierTier 1Tier 2Tier 3Tier 4Tier 5
    Total XP103060100150

    Combat perks show (Attribute/Attribute) for XP contribution when purchased. Combat perks may require weapon training and/or wielding specific weapons: Training Requirements:

    • “Category 1/Category 2” - Requires training in Category 1 OR Category 2
    • “Category 1+Category 2” - Requires training in BOTH Category 1 AND Category 2

    Using the Perk: To use a perk, you must be wielding a weapon from one of the required categories (or both, if the perk requires multiple weapons simultaneously).

    6. Magic System

    6. Magic System

    Note: The balance is not yet here. As per current state there will be some issues with spells having on average higher rolls vs defense rolls.

    Core Principles

    Magic can only be cast by Mages - people with inherited or acquired magical ability.

    If you want to be a Mage, take the appropriate perk (requires GM permission) and select your origin spell (Basic).

    Magic in Exceed can beAttuned or not:

    • Attuned magic: Uses the Limit system, is persistent, requires no actions to maintain
    • Active spells: Require a [Casting Check]: Spellcraft + Wit + Bonuses vs DC [8 + Tier × 2]
    • A spell can be bothActive andAttuned (e.g. summoning: you cast it with AP and it sustains on a Limit slot)
    • A spell can be neither (no tag) Magic has no fuel pool - its cost is risk: failed casts accumulate Strain until a breather is taken.

    Strain

    Strain is the cost of overreaching magic: failed casts accumulate it, strain makes further casting harder, and past the limit it becomes wounds.

    1. Failed [Casting Check] of a Tier 1+ spell → +1 Strain. Tier 0 spells don’t cause Strain but are affected by it.
    2. Strain effect: adds +Strain to all casting DCs (max 5; the modifier stays +5 while at Strain 5).
    3. Gaining the 5th Strain → roll a [Consequence Roll] counted as non-lethal: Critical failures work as if taken crirical hits and add +10.
    4. Gaining Strain while already at 5 → roll a normal [Consequence Roll], it is counted as normal wound for all intents and purpuses.
    5. 1 Breather → all Strain clears. Wounds caused by strain recover under normal wound rules - see Severity Levels and Recovery Rules.

    Note: Wounds gained from strain can lead to character death as all normal wounds.

    Limit System

    Limit represents your capacity to sustain persistent magical and supernatural effects.

    Base Limit

    Limit = 3 + Will + Spellcraft

    What Counts Against Limit

    Everything with a Limit[N] trait. This usually includes:

    • Personal magical buffs and enchantments (Shield, Invisibility, Enhanced Strength)
    • Communal effects affecting self + allies (Group Fire Resistance, Party Water Walking)
    • Summoned creatures and constructs
    • Active magical items requiring Limit slots (enchanted armor, magical weapons)

    Managing Limit

    • Attuned magic is generally configured outside of combat and requires no roll
    • Swapping in combat: 1 action for 1 effect, or 3 actions for all effects
    • Multiple instances of the same effect do not stack - only the strongest version applies

    Teams

    Mages can conduct a simple breather-long Team Ritual with willing targets to form a team. This allows:

    • Mage can apply team effects on allies while they are in effect reach
    • Allies lose effects when exiting range and regain them when entering
    • Mage can cast harmful effects on allies bypassing Endure check

    To break away from a team: Spend a full round on your turn and succeed a Will roll against the ritualist’s Magical Theory skill.

    Metamagic

    Metamagic lets a caster push a spell beyond its baseline at the cost of a harder, more expensive cast. A master doesn’t struggle with simple spells — they make simple spells do more.

    Rules

    9.1 Time and Travel

    Exceed Time

    Time in Exceed is measured using a power of 10 system starting with heartbeats and ending with days. Time units are:

    UnitDurationReference and terms
    HearbeatLess then a secondBeat, instant
    Round10 Hearbeats9s, a short moment
    Minuta10 Rounds90s, a short task
    Breather10 Minutas15m, Quick rest
    Shift10 BreathersA work/study shifts
    Day10 ShiftsFrom dawn to next
    Tenday10 Days2 ‘work weeks’
    Season10 Tendays(100d)1/3 of a year
    When abilities mention time change by magnitude - move the unit up or down by the magnitude. E.g. wound that needs a season to recover with aid will recover in a Tenday.
    In the world of Exceed, towns usually have full-day clocks that ring bells every half a shift after dawn, and workplaces have shift clocks, with the last section of a shift being a usual breather time.

    Full night’s rest requires 3 Shifts.

    Travel Speed

    Travel Speed

    Each character has a Travel Speed, equal to their Speed –Exploration activities for that Shift.
    Use the lowest Travel Speed in the party when calculating group travel.

    Apply Route Difficulty as follows:

    • Open Route: Travel Speed × 3 km
    • Moderate Route: Travel Speed × 2 km
    • Strenuous Route: Travel Speed × 1 km
    • Very Strenuous Route: Cannot be traveled without first reducing its difficulty.

    Reducing Route Difficulty

    Some abilities, skills, and spells can help you overcome harsh routes.
    Effects from the same source (such as a single spell, perk, or exploration activity) stack, while different sources do not, allowing you to reduce the route penalty multiple times when using related effects.

    Marching and Fatigue

    You can make up to three Marches per day without a check.
    Each additional March beyond the third requires a DC 10 Endure or Running check.

    • Increase the DC by +5 for each consecutive extra March that day.
    • On failure, you gain the Fatigued condition.
    • You cannot Force March while Fatigued.

    Forced March

    A Forced March doubles your travel distance for one March (×2) but counts as two Marches toward your daily limit.
    During this, no character can perform more than one Exploration activity.

    If you fail the fatigue check, the Forced March becomes a normal March, and you gain the Fatigued condition at the end of it.

    Example

    If you march three times and then continue, the check DC is 10.
    If you choose to Force March instead, the DC is 15.

    Earth time Variant rule

    Time in Exceed can be counted as normal, with rounds, minutes, hours, and weeks. But is intended to be used with Exceed’s time system.     Recovery Rules

    Sleep

    A Night’s Rest consists of 3 or more Shifts (~8 hours). Each character requires at least 3 Shifts of sleep within a Day to recover normally.

    Sleep Deprivation and Fatigue

    • Going a full Day without sleep: Gain 1 level of Fatigue, plus +1 for every 5 Shifts (~12 hours) thereafter
    • Fatigue from lack of sleep cannot be removed until completing a full extended rest of 3 + Fatigue Level Shifts
    • Magical or alchemical solutions may delay effects or temporarily reduce Fatigue, but do not remove accumulated fatigue

    Recovering from Fatigue

    • Each Rest Shift removes 1 level of Fatigue (except fatigue from sleep deprivation)
    • Comfortable rest, proper food, or medical care may remove an additional level per Night’s Rest (GM discretion)
    • Characters cannot recover from Fatigue while marching, exploring, or in combat

    Downtime Activities During Recovery

    During recovery, characters may engage in light downtime activities such as learning or income work, as long as the GM judges these do not meaningfully interrupt rest.

    9.3 Exploration and Out of combat Activities

    Circumvent Security Device

    Traits: —

    This activity allows a character to disarm traps, pick locks, bypass wards, or overcome similar security devices.
    Each device defines:

    • Successes required
    • Time per attempt
    • Applicable skills
    • Consequences of failure

    How It Works

    When attempting to circumvent a device, roll the relevant skill once per attempt.
    Each success counts toward the total number required.
    Unless stated otherwise:

    • A failure wastes the time spent.
    • A critical failure escalates the situation at the GM’s discretion (jammed lock, triggered trap, etc.).

    Examples

    Average Lock

    • Successes Required: 1
    • Time per Attempt: 1 Minuta
    • Skills: Lock-picking, Breaking
    • Consequence: None — the attempt simply fails.

    Alarming Ward

    • Successes Required: 2
    • Time per Attempt: 1 Breather
    • Skills: Magical Theory; Lock-picking (–2 penalty)
    • Consequence: On failure, the magical alarm triggers.

    High-End Wall Safe

    • Successes Required: 3
    • Time per Attempt: 1 Shift
    • Skills: Lock-picking, Breaking
    • Consequence:
      • On failure, the lock jams, increasing the total successes required by +1 (maximum 5).
      • On critical failure, the safe becomes jammed, requiring at least a 4 shifts worth of repair.

    Forage

    Traits:Exploration , Speed Penalty 1. You try to gather food, herbs or materials. The DC is static based on the nature of the place and availability of resources. Skill is dependent on the environment and the target of foraging, usually being Biology for herbs, survival for food and water and burning materials in wilderness, streetwise for in city environments.

    Each attempt at Foraging takes one Shift. You can forage on the go, during marching, camping etc by lowering your speed by 1 and increases the DC by 2. Number of rations/materials foraged are loosely rules by the GM.

    Critical effects

    On a critical success you find double the amount or something extra up to GM. On a critical failure you encounter possible trouble - misidentify the food, get predator’s attention, or similar.

    Examples for Game Masters

    Lush forest

    • DC to forage food is 10. Herbs is 12. Materials is Up to GM.

    • Food foraging will provide 3 rations on success.

    • Herbs will provide 3 batches. Hillside

    • DC to forage food is 12. Herbs is 14. Materials is Up to GM.

    • Food foraging will provide 2 rations on success.

    • Herbs will provide 2 batches. Mountain

    • DC to forage food is 14. Herbs is 16. Materials is Up to GM.

    • Food foraging will provide 2 rations on success.

    • Herbs will provide 2 batches. Deserted area

    • DC to forage food is 16. Herbs is 20. Materials is Up to GM.

    • Food foraging will provide 1 rations on success.

    • Herbs will provide 1 batches.
