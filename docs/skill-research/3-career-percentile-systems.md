# Career & percentile systems on skills

*Research report for the ExceedV skill rework. Sources: Chaosium free quick-start PDFs (RuneQuest Glorantha, Pendragon 6e, CoC 7e) parsed directly; WFRP4e FoundryVTT system docs (rules-accurate on career tiers, advance caps, switching costs); Wikipedia system sections; RPG Stackexchange for Shadowrun specifics.*

## 1. RuneQuest / BRP — layered identity, improvement by *use*

Every skill starts from a **base chance** plus **skill category modifiers** (agility, communication, knowledge, magic, manipulation, perception, stealth — derived from characteristics, already baked into sheet values). Character built in layers — **Homeland → Culture → Occupation → Cult** — each contributing skill packages, not points. Cults have three ranks (lay / initiate / Rune level); **Runes** come in opposed pairs summing to 100% and gate Rune-magic casting chance; **Passions** (Loyalty, Devotion, Hate, Love, Honor, Fear) are rated like skills and **augment** one roll per scene. Improvement is pure use-based: mark an experience check on a meaningful success; at session's end roll d100 **over** your skill to gain a few percent — the better you are, the harder to improve. Training can be bought up to a cap.

## 2. Call of Cthulhu 7e — the occupation *is* the build

No point-buy: the occupation grants a **curated skill list** (pick ~8 skills), funded by **occupation skill points** (EDU×4, or hybrid formulas). **Credit Rating** — money and lifestyle — *is a skill bought with occupation points*: profession literally prices your place in society. Separate breadth valve: **personal interest points = INT×2**, spendable on any skill. Growth: fail a roll in play, then at session end roll d100 over the skill for +1D10. Profession-fantasy answered through **list curation + a wealth gate**, not tree structure.

## 3. Pendragon — paired traits and passions, Glory as advancement

d20 roll-under. Thirteen **opposed Trait pairs** (Chaste/Lustful, Valorous/Cowardly…) split 1–20, plus **Passions** (Loyalty, Love, Hospitality, Honor, Hate, Station) rated ~3–18. Passions invoke → roll → **bonus to a single Skill or Trait for the scene** (once per day per Passion); failure cascades Melancholy → Madness. **Glory** — the XP analog — awarded only for great deeds; totals gate social rank, retinues, titles; Honor loss costs knighthood. Skills grow through seasonal use, resolved in the annual **Winter Phase**. High personality values become identity *rules*: a **Famous (16+) Trait must always be rolled first**; an **Exalted (20) Trait never fails**.

## 4. WFRP 4e — careers as structure (the flagship profession game)

Careers are 4-tier ladders (**Aide → Advisor → Counsellor → Chancellor**) inside career groups across eight classes. Each tier defines: available **Characteristics**, 8 **Career Skills** (each tier includes all prior tiers' skills), **Career Talents**, and **Trappings** — the tools of the trade you must own to count as the profession. One **Current career** at a time: XP buys advances only on the current career's lists — skills at +5% for 5 XP flat, characteristics escalating 25→45 XP, talents ~100 XP — capped at **5 × career tier** advances per item. Non-career skills raisable at **double cost**. **Completing** a tier (advances bought + trappings held) is a manual milestone; switching careers early costs **+100 XP**. Specialization happens *inside* career skills: "Melee (Any) becomes Melee (Polearm)." Careers don't grant competence — they define what's cheap, what's capped, in what order.

## 5. Shadowrun — attribute+skill pools, specialization as cheap tag

Since 4e: dice pool = **Attribute + Skill**, hits on 5–6. Creation priority-based or karma buy. Ratings ~1–13 with **escalating karma costs** ("skills already well-developed cost more"), encouraging specialization. **Specializations are flat-cheap tags**: 1 skill point at creation, 7 Karma after, for +2 dice in the narrow case. **Knowledge skills** are a separate cheaper freeform-named pool — breadth affordable, active depth dear. Archetypes (street samurai, decker) are emergent skill+gear combos, explicitly *not classes*: "restrictions are not imposed by the system itself."

## 6. World/Chronicles of Darkness — dots, specialties, fields

Attributes and Skills 0–5 **dots** in a 3×3 grid (Mental/Physical/Social × 24 skills); pool = Attribute + Skill, success at 8+. Skills may be zero — untrained use defaults at a penalty. **Specialties** are the specialization layer: free named dots at creation (three in CofD; one free on the 4th dot in 1e) giving **+1 die** when applicable. **Fields**: knowledge skills carry a declared field — dots measure depth, the field names the domain — so "what you know" is legible without inflating the list. Escalating per-dot XP.

## Answers

- **(a) Professions vs point-buy:** three models — *template overlay* (CoC occupation, RQG homeland/culture/cult packages), *profession as price structure* (WFRP careers gate cheap/capped/order), *pure point-buy with emergent archetypes* (SR, CofD).
- **(b) breadth vs depth:** CoC linear; SR/CofD/WFRP escalate depth while breadth stays cheap (SR knowledge skills, CoC INT×2 personal interest, WFRP non-career at only 2×).
- **(c) specialization:** *cheap tags on real skills* (+2 dice, +1 die) — SR specializations, CofD specialties — or in-career choices (WFRP Melee (Polearm)); never separate skills.
- **(d) use-based vs point-buy:** use-growth rewards risk, needs no economy, but is random, slow, conservative-play-inducing; WFRP/SR/CofD buy determinism. Nobody mixes at the same granularity.
- **(e) what levels gate beyond rolls:** Credit Rating (lifestyle), WFRP trappings + tier completion (career progression), SR technique thresholds, RQ Rune % (casting), Pendragon Glory totals (rank) and Famous/Exalted traits (compelled behavior), CofD Merit prerequisites.

## 5 structural ideas worth stealing

1. **Profession = curated starting package, not a tree node** (CoC): kills the synthesis-node problem.
2. **Career milestones over free point-buy** (WFRP): "complete levels + own trappings" unlocks the next rank — narrative spine without classes.
3. **Escalating depth, near-free breadth tags**: cheap 1st levels, dear level 5; specializations as 1 XP tags granting +2 or a reroll inside a niche.
3. **A rated Passions track** (RQG/Pendragon): 3–5 bonds that once per scene augment any skill — moves identity math off the skill list.
5. **Every skill level gates something**: a perk, a wealth/rank threshold, a social standing — no gated payoff, no skill on the list.

## 3 traps to avoid

1. **Parameterized skill families without adjacency** — GURPS families work because types cross-default; a family with shared-or-separate levels and no cross-discount fragments pricing.
2. **Improvement-by-use bookkeeping** — random growth, session-end ceremony; a point-buy game gains nothing.
3. **Long flat lists doing the profession-fantasy work** — CoC/WFRP afford 60–200 skills *because professions curate them*; a small point-buy game curates via packages instead.
